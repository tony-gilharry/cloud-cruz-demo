# 06 Credentials

**A Cloud Cruz credential proves a customer result, not course attendance. Partners climb five steps, from an accreditation in the first weeks to an advisor who runs the Readiness Review. Each step unlocks something real: a tier, the implementation package, or the right to deliver.** (Decisions 22, 27, 47, 48, 52, 53, 64, 80, 81, 83)

> All of this is synthetic. Cloud Cruz and its partners are invented.

## The ladder

Badges are issued at the lifecycle stage where they're earned (decision 27). Levels 2 and 3 split into a **technical track** (hands-on implementation) and a **business track** (sales and project leads).

| Step | Stage | Technical track | Business track | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales Accreditation** | Onboard | | Product, pricing plans and how to partner with Cloud Cruz | Counts toward onboarded and Select |
| **Technical Accreditation** | Onboard | Platform basics in the sandbox | | Counts toward onboarded and Select |
| **Level 1: Foundations** | Enable | Exam plus a sandbox lab: connect a source, ship a first query | Required of everyone: the value proposition, co-positioning, and a joint plan to win a real target customer on the Problem Statement Canvas | The entry point for Levels 2 and 3 |
| **Level 2: Delivery** | Enable | 2 customer go-lives on the 30-day Launch plan, with customer sign-off and a usage event | 2 signed business cases, with a baseline and a target ROI confirmed at 90 days | The implementation package and the right to deliver the Launch plan; counts toward Premier |
| **Level 3: Advisor** | Enable | 3 Readiness Reviews run, with high-risk items closed | 3 accounts expanded on proven ROI, plus executive value reviews | Counts toward Elite |

**Why technical people take the business Level 1:** they co-sell in pods, so they need to explain the problem their own firm solves and why Cloud Cruz (decision 52).

**The Problem Statement Canvas** is a public framework (Marius Ursache, Disciplined Entrepreneurship Toolbox, 2018). It covers context, customer type, root problem, emotional impact, quantifiable impact, and alternatives and their disadvantages. The partner fills it in for a real customer it wants to win.

## The Readiness Review

The Level 3 technical advisor runs a one-page review of a customer's Cloud Cruz setup on four pillars (decision 64). The format follows a common public architecture-review pattern: a fixed rubric, and high-risk items become the fix list.

| Pillar | The question |
|---|---|
| **Trust** | Is the data good, secure and reliable? |
| **AI quality** | Are the agent's answers accurate, and are they evaluated? |
| **Cost** | Will the bill surprise anyone? |
| **Adoption** | Are people actually using it? |

## Outcomes, not claims

Every Level 2 and Level 3 result is measured against the customer's own baseline from kickoff, and the customer signs off (decision 52). A go-live counts when Cloud Cruz sees a usage event, not when a partner says so.

## When a credential counts

- **Accreditations don't expire** (decision 80). They mark onboarding, and the 90-day product-use rule below already keeps inactive people from counting.
- **Level 1 and above last 12 months.** Partners recertify on major releases (decision 47).
- **A person counts toward a tier only with an active credential and hands-on product use in the last 90 days** (decision 47). Knowledge fades, and a badge shouldn't outlive the skill.
- **The implementation package needs at least one Level 2 technical person on the deal** (decision 53). During the 90-day beta, Cloud Cruz's partner solutions architect stands in, and the partner's people earn Level 2 from those go-lives (decision 91).

## What it costs partners

Courses, labs and both accreditations are free. Level exams carry a fee, and every tier gets free exams each program year to cover it (decisions 48, 81):

| Exam | Fee |
|---|---|
| Level 1: Foundations | $100 |
| Level 2: Delivery | $150 |
| Level 3: Advisor | $150 |

| Tier | Free exams a year |
|---|---|
| Member | 5 |
| Select | 10 |
| Premier | 10 |
| Elite | 15 |

Free exams work at any level, recertification included, and reset every Apr 1, the launch anniversary. Anchor partners also get custom sessions and extra vouchers for large cohorts (decision 48). **People on the accelerated onboarding plan get extra vouchers** as an incentive to take the course and certify quickly, issued automatically so it runs for every partner on that plan (decisions 40, 81).

> **Internal note: why exams carry a fee.** A free exam doesn't get taken seriously. People sit it without preparing, fail, and a second try means little too. A priced exam gets focus, especially when a person's company is paying or reimbursing it, so people prepare and try harder to pass. The vouchers make the exam free to the partner in practice, while it keeps a price, and a credential keeps its value.


## Systems

- **Credly** issues badges in production, so partners get Cloud Cruz badges in the same wallet as their other cloud credentials (decision 22).
- **Certifier** issues badges in the demo (decision 27).
- **introw** hosts the courses, study material and the two accreditations.
- **Pearson VUE** proctors the Level 1, 2 and 3 exams and issues the vouchers; a pass triggers the Credly badge (decision 83).
- **Salesforce** keeps a Credential record per person, with level, track, issue date, expiry and last product use, so tier reviews read one source.

## Why this saves time and money

Customers get partners who have already delivered, so fewer projects stall and fewer need rescue. Cloud Cruz spends its implementation package only where a proven Level 2 person is on the deal. Partners learn for free, most never pay for an exam, and they know exactly what each level takes.
